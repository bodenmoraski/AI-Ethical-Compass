# Analysis: Current Questions vs. Better Approach

## Executive Summary

**The Problem:** Step 2 ("Identify AI Use") is a comprehension question masquerading as an ethics question. It doesn't advance ethical thinking and often feels odd or confusing.

**The Solution:** Replace it with a "Core Ethical Dilemma" step that forces students to grapple with competing values specific to each scenario.

---

## Current 5-Step Flow

1. **Read the Scenario** ✅ Good
2. **Identify AI Use** ❌ **Problem**
3. **Evaluate Ethics** ✅ Good (but comes too late)
4. **Share Perspective** ✅ Good
5. **Explore Community** ✅ Good

---

## Why "Identify AI Use" Doesn't Work

### Issue #1: It's a Reading Comprehension Question, Not an Ethics Question

**Example from AI-Generated Essay:**
- "Yes - The student likely used an AI text generator to write the entire essay"
- "Yes - The student may have used AI to help edit or enhance their own writing"
- "Not sure - There's insufficient information to determine AI use"

**The Problem:** This is asking students to **speculate about facts** we can't know, not to **reason about ethics**. The actual ethical dilemma isn't WHETHER AI was used—it's HOW a teacher should respond given uncertainty while supporting ESL students.

### Issue #2: Some Options Are Technical Trivia Questions

**Example from Facial Recognition:**
- "Yes - Facial recognition is a form of AI used for identity verification"
- "No - This is standard security technology without advanced AI"

**The Problem:** This tests whether students know if facial recognition counts as "AI"—a definitional debate that has nothing to do with the ethical issues of surveillance, bias, and privacy.

### Issue #3: It Doesn't Prepare Students for the Real Dilemma

The "Identify AI Use" step doesn't set up the ethical tension students need to grapple with. Instead, they get to Step 3 (Evaluate Ethics) without having thought through the core tradeoffs.

### Issue #4: The Options Conflate Different Issues

Looking at the options across scenarios, they mix:
- Technical classification ("Is this AI?")
- Factual speculation ("Did someone use AI?")
- Degree of confidence ("Yes / Partially / Not sure")
- Implementation details ("AI with human oversight?")

These aren't ethically meaningful distinctions.

---

## What Step 2 SHOULD Be: Core Ethical Dilemma

Replace "Identify AI Use" with a question that:

✅ **Identifies the central ethical tension/tradeoff in the scenario**  
✅ **Has multiple defensible positions (no single "right" answer)**  
✅ **Requires weighing competing values**  
✅ **Is specific to each scenario**  
✅ **Prepares students for deeper ethical analysis in Step 3**

---

## Proposed Better Questions for Each Scenario

### Scenario 1: AI-Generated Essay

**❌ Current Question:** "How do you think AI is being used in this scenario?"

**✅ Better Question:** "What should be the teacher's PRIMARY priority in this situation?"

**Options:**
1. **Academic Integrity First** - "Maintaining academic standards and ensuring work is authentic, even if it means confronting the student"  
   *Core value: Fairness to other students, learning outcomes*

2. **Supporting the Student** - "Understanding the student's challenges as an ESL learner and avoiding accusations without proof"  
   *Core value: Equity, avoiding harm, presumption of innocence*

3. **Gathering More Information** - "Investigating further before making any decisions about the student's work"  
   *Core value: Due process, evidence-based decision making*

4. **Rethinking Assessment** - "Adapting assignments and policies to acknowledge that AI tools are now part of writing"  
   *Core value: Adapting education to technological reality*

5. **Balanced Approach** - "Finding a way to both uphold standards and support the student's growth"  
   *Core value: Multiple stakeholder interests*

**Why This Works:** Forces students to identify WHICH ethical principle should take precedence, setting up the nuanced discussion in later steps.

---

### Scenario 2: Facial Recognition in Schools

**❌ Current Question:** "How do you think AI is being used in this scenario?"

**✅ Better Question:** "What should be the deciding factor in whether to implement facial recognition?"

**Options:**
1. **Security Effectiveness** - "If it genuinely improves school safety, the privacy concerns are worth addressing"  
   *Core value: Student safety, security*

2. **Privacy Rights** - "Student privacy and avoiding surveillance should be the priority, regardless of security benefits"  
   *Core value: Privacy, freedom from surveillance*

3. **Bias and Equity** - "The system should only be used if it works equally well for all students, regardless of skin tone"  
   *Core value: Fairness, avoiding discrimination*

4. **Consent and Choice** - "Students and families should have the option to opt-in or opt-out of the system"  
   *Core value: Autonomy, informed consent*

5. **Cost-Benefit Analysis** - "Compare costs, effectiveness, and risks against alternative security approaches"  
   *Core value: Practical effectiveness, resource allocation*

**Why This Works:** Gets at the core tradeoff (security vs. privacy vs. bias concerns) without asking the pointless "Is this AI?" question.

---

### Scenario 3: AI Content Moderation

**❌ Current Question:** "How do you think AI is being used in this scenario?"

**✅ Better Question:** "What should the platform prioritize in content moderation?"

**Options:**
1. **Student Safety First** - "Aggressive moderation to ensure no harmful content, even if some legitimate posts get removed"  
   *Core value: Protection from harm, creating safe spaces*

2. **Academic Freedom** - "Allowing open discussion of difficult topics, even if it means some inappropriate content slips through"  
   *Core value: Free speech, intellectual exploration*

3. **Accuracy Over Speed** - "Human review of all flagged content before removal, even if it causes delays"  
   *Core value: Due process, accuracy*

4. **Context Awareness** - "Investing in better AI that understands educational context, accepting current limitations"  
   *Core value: Technological improvement, nuance*

5. **Teacher Authority** - "Letting teachers set moderation levels for their own class discussions"  
   *Core value: Professional judgment, local control*

**Why This Works:** The dilemma isn't "Is this AI?"—it's the classic tension between safety and freedom in educational discourse.

---

### Scenario 4: AI in College Admissions

**❌ Current Question:** "How do you think AI is being used in this scenario?"

**✅ Better Question:** "What should be the primary concern about AI-assisted admissions?"

**Options:**
1. **Algorithmic Bias** - "The system may disadvantage certain groups even without explicit bias in design"  
   *Core value: Fairness, equity*

2. **Transparency** - "Students have a right to know how they're being evaluated and by what criteria"  
   *Core value: Explainability, informed process*

3. **Human Judgment** - "Important life-changing decisions shouldn't be delegated to algorithms"  
   *Core value: Human dignity, personal assessment*

4. **Efficiency Gains** - "AI helps identify deserving students who'd be missed in manual review, benefits outweigh risks"  
   *Core value: Effectiveness, discovering hidden talent*

5. **Data Quality** - "The AI is only as good as its training data; garbage in, garbage out"  
   *Core value: Evidence-based decision making, validity*

**Why This Works:** Moves beyond "Is this AI?" to the actual ethical tensions in algorithmic decision-making about human futures.

---

### Scenario 5: Accessibility AI Tools (Example)

**✅ Better Question:** "What should guide decisions about AI accessibility tools in testing?"

**Options:**
1. **Leveling the Playing Field** - "Tools that help students with disabilities demonstrate their knowledge are essential accommodations"
2. **Academic Standards** - "Accommodations shouldn't fundamentally change what's being assessed"
3. **Universal Design** - "Tests should be designed so all students can access them without AI tools"
4. **Individual Needs** - "Each student's situation is unique and requires personalized assessment of appropriate tools"
5. **Consistency** - "All students should have access to the same tools to ensure fairness"

## Benefits of This Approach

### 1. **Genuine Ethical Reasoning**
Students must think about values, tradeoffs, and priorities—not just "Is this AI?"

### 2. **Better Preparation for Discussion**
By identifying the core dilemma in Step 2, students are primed for deeper analysis in Steps 3-4.

### 3. **No "Right" Answer**
All options are defensible, encouraging diverse perspectives rather than quiz-like "correct" answers.

### 4. **Scenario-Specific**
Each question is tailored to the actual ethical tensions in that specific situation.

### 5. **Clearer Learning Objectives**
Directly aligns with critical thinking about ethical frameworks, stakeholder analysis, and value prioritization.

### 6. **Addresses Dave's Feedback**
Makes questions clearer, less vague, and more conducive to proper analysis.

## Additional Scenario Detail Improvements

While we're at it, here are ways to make scenarios more detailed (per Dave's feedback):

### Add These Elements to Each Scenario:

1. **Specific Names & Context**
   - Current: "A high school teacher"
   - Better: "Ms. Rodriguez, a 10th-grade English teacher at Lincoln High (1,200 students, 40% ESL)"

2. **Concrete Details**
   - Current: "The essay seems unusually sophisticated"
   - Better: "The essay analyzes Shakespeare with graduate-level sophistication, includes 15 correctly formatted MLA citations, uses terminology like 'intertextual dialectic'"

3. **Stakes & Timeline**
   - Current: "The assignment was a crucial part of the semester grade"
   - Better: "The essay represents 30% of the semester grade and is due November 15th, three weeks before college applications"

4. **Stakeholder Perspectives**
   - Add brief quotes or viewpoints from different stakeholders
   - "The student's mother: 'My son worked so hard on this. Why are you accusing him?'"
   - "Another teacher: 'We need to take a stand on AI or academic integrity means nothing.'"

5. **Technical Specificity**
   - Current: "Facial recognition technology"
   - Better: "FaceGuard Pro system that uses infrared scanning and neural networks, storing biometric data for 5 years"
